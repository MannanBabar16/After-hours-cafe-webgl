# Coffee Craft & Café Life — design notes

The fantasy is learning a craft, making a living and becoming someone’s favorite place. Coffee quality feeds tips and familiarity; earnings feed stock, décor and eventual ownership. Guided play keeps the first evening readable. Optional depth lives in dials, the café desk and practice.

## The playable loop

Guest arrives → quoted order → select beans and recipe → grind / prepare / extract → milk or water finish → taste feedback → carry / serve → sale, tip, XP and relationships → restock / customize → close / review profit → next evening.

The next action is always visible. Guests wait patiently. Early closing makes sessions flexible. Guided mode handles the level tamp and shot stop; hands-on mode allows experimentation with the same underlying flow model. No menus need to be mastered before the first espresso.

## What the player learns

| Action               | Immediate result                       | Transferable idea                           |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Change grind         | Different shot speed                   | Water needs appropriate resistance          |
| Change dose          | Different target yield and flow        | Keep dose and ratio coherent                |
| Distribute and tamp  | Puck consistency affects quality       | Even preparation reduces channeling         |
| Stop extraction      | Yield, time and tasting feedback       | Judge a shot using multiple signals         |
| Texture milk         | Temperature, quality and tip potential | Air first, then swirl; avoid excessive heat |
| Control the pour     | Surface pattern and score              | Integrate first, then pour low and steady   |
| Flush and wipe       | Clean bar and restored consistency     | Hygiene is part of craft                    |
| Compare journal cups | Personal best and written feedback     | Change one variable at a time               |

This is an educational abstraction, not a physical espresso simulator. Flavor feedback is a starting hypothesis; actual beans, water, machines and taste complicate it. The timed tamp stands in for level pressure. Latte-art patterns illustrate a finish rather than teaching hand movement in full detail.

## A café that feels like a business

Stock is finite, waste has a cost and supplies require cash. The P&L separates profit from available money. Pricing policies trade arrival pace against margin and quality expectations. Croissant pairings add basket value with little extra complexity. Regulars tip more. Dirty tables slow arrivals; buying Mochi trades capital for less cleanup.

Three evening goals reward service, quality and sales. They pay once, automatically. XP grows from actual service; practice earns skill feedback rather than cash or progression. Recipes unlock gradually: Espresso at the start, Latte after one service, Cappuccino after three, Americano after eight lifetime cups, Flat white after fourteen. Save $500 to own the café and remove rent. The long-term dream can be reached through the implemented economy.

## Expressing a place

Free changes give instant ownership: a café name, five apron colors, five cup finishes and three wall moods. Paid décor makes earnings visible in the room. Six guests retain distinct interests and preferred styles. Milo, Mochi, rain, social posts and familiar greetings make a shift feel inhabited.

## Feedback worth carrying into the original game

1. **Put the lesson inside the action.** Let the player hear a grinder, see a yield and taste a result before presenting a long explanation.
2. **Connect craft to money visibly.** A receipt that separates coffee, pastry and tip explains why a beautiful cup matters.
3. **Make failure informative.** Sour, bitter, cool or hot feedback should suggest one next experiment. Avoid random quality ratings.
4. **Keep practice safe.** A quiet lab with recorded experiments makes a deeper simulation approachable.
5. **Separate skill from administration.** Finish a cup at the bar; make stock and pricing decisions at a calm desk.
6. **Let investments solve a felt problem.** A cleaning helper matters after the player has cleared real used tables.
7. **Build goals at several scales.** One cup, tonight’s targets, another recipe, a regular, an upgraded room, eventual ownership.
8. **Reward consistency more than speed.** A calm café can still have mastery, planning and a satisfying economy.

## Next experiments for a larger original game

These are development directions, not features included in this browser build:

- A tactile portafilter and steam-wand interaction with forgiving mouse/controller gestures.
- An actual aeration-versus-vortex milk simulation with audible bubble size and feedback.
- A weekly supplier board: freshness, bean origin, fluctuating costs and delivery lead times.
- Customer flavor preferences, custom orders and remembered favorite bean profiles.
- Meaningful machine upgrades: temperature stability, grinder retention and capacity.
- A small staff roster with training, wages and delegation; introduce it only after solo service feels good.
- Bakery preparation as a second craft loop, added after the core coffee loop is polished.
- A few authored story evenings, festivals and visiting critics with optional challenges.
- Freeform furnishing with clear aisle/capacity checks and a layout preview.
- A weekly balance sheet and business milestones, with gentle recovery options.
- Controller-first interaction, accessibility settings and a tested Steam build pipeline.

Test each addition against a concrete question: does it improve the feeling of making coffee, running the café or knowing the people? Add one system at a time and watch players’ first ten minutes before widening the scope.
